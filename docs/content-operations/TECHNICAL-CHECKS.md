# Technical terminology and decision-usefulness checks

Read this check before drafting or revising engineering content. It supplements the company facts and specialist prompt. It does not certify a design or replace the actual drawing review.

- Separate **thread runout region** (incomplete thread near an end), **thread relief / undercut** (a deliberate reduced section), and **circular / total runout** (a datum-based geometric tolerance). An axial incomplete-thread length is not a geometric-runout tolerance. Define the term before specifying its inspection.
- Plain-feature size tolerances such as g6 or h7 and thread designations such as 6g or 6H refer to different interfaces. Preserve case, order and the actual drawing system. A size tolerance alone does not mandate one grinding process.
- Distinguish material grade, product form and condition from finished-fastener property class and assembled-joint performance. Label nominal, minimum and typical values accurately. Do not infer every screw's strength from a stock-material number.
- Treat torque, friction, thread engagement, mating material and installation as joint requirements. Do not invent a universal torque, holding force, safe lifting load, pressure rating or fatigue life.
- Flat, cone, dog and cup points have different contact mechanisms. Flat does not guarantee no marking; a locating extension is not automatically an approved shear connection. Compare the actual mating surface and intended adjustment sequence.
- A visible-profile CCD result is evidence only for the validated characteristics and setup. It is not proof of material grade, hardness, every thread characteristic, hidden geometry or all datum relationships.
- Give a reader an actionable comparison, filled example or decision sequence when the title promises selection or planning help. A list of things to ask the supplier is insufficient when the actual question is which alternative to choose. Do not impose a word-count target or the same section template on every article.
- Label examples, planning records and sketches as such. Do not present blank worksheets as completed inspections, reference photographs as proof of tolerances, or generated illustrations as actual installations or tests.
- Explain the actual technical distinction rather than padding the answer with repeated caveats. Keep qualifications next to the claim they limit.
- Consult the existing product, material and process pages before adding an explanation. Prefer a useful missing comparison, calculation or inspection record over another paragraph telling the reader to confirm the drawing. Keep related pages complementary: a product page helps specify and inquire; a guide explains the decision in depth.
- Keep worked inputs, units and assumptions together. Check the arithmetic, both tolerance extremes and the meaning of the result. A cost crossover is conditional on the stated costs and production horizon; a clearance calculation is not validation of the complete joint.
- Localize technical intent, not English word order. In a drawing review, “review” means assess the requirements, not look back at a previous event. Use a stable glossary for shoulder, bore, tooling, captive retention and finished condition, and keep the same numeric assumptions in every language.
- PAA, keyword estimates, source availability and third-party audit scores are editorial references, not publication prerequisites. Do not invent observed PAA or ranking metrics. The decision aids illustrate useful approaches, not a mandatory format for every article.
- External references remain optional. Verify consequential technical assertions against approved information or a suitable primary technical reference when available. Omit or qualify an unverified precise claim; do not invent a citation, certification, standard clause or reviewer identity.
- A writing-style risk score or SEO score does not establish engineering accuracy. Use an honest editorial byline; only name a technical reviewer and their experience when verified.

Terminology references for editorial checking: KEYENCE GD&T Fundamentals, Run-out Tolerance; Unbrako Engineering Guide, set-screw point geometry. These references do not establish ZUFEK manufacturing capability or order-specific acceptance limits.
