Choose the shoulder around its actual function

A locating shoulder, a rotating pivot and a spacing stop impose different requirements. For a pivot, examine the mating bore, running clearance and contact length. For a spacer, the shoulder length controls the clamped stack. For a locating feature, identify the datum axis and the surfaces that establish position. A close diameter tolerance alone does not resolve these different functions.

Check the axial stack before tightening

Consider a cover rotating on a shoulder screw. If the shoulder ends below the cover stack, tightening may clamp the moving part. If it projects too far, excessive axial play can remain. Show the complete stack, washers and seating face on the assembly drawing; then dimension the shoulder length against the intended clearance rather than copying the overall screw length. This is an illustrative design review, not a released customer design.

Protect the transition and final fit

The shoulder-to-thread transition needs enough clearance for the mating part to seat. Specify usable thread engagement separately from the visible threaded length and show any relief. Review straightness, runout and surface texture where these affect motion. If heat treatment or coating follows machining, establish which shoulder dimensions are accepted in the final supplied condition and whether finishing allowance is needed.

Select the route and verify the result

CNC turning is a route to review for sample quantities and complex steps. Cold heading followed by local machining can be evaluated for repeat production. Grinding is selected where size, form or surface requirements justify it, rather than assumed from the product name. CCD inspection can screen accessible profiles; datum relationships, surface texture and thread acceptance need their own suitable methods.

From drawing to approved samples

Provide the drawing, material, finish and intended assembly. Identify the drawing revision, prototype quantity and expected repeat demand. Where the first part is based on an existing sample, separately confirm material and hidden features rather than inferring them from appearance.

The typical sampling lead time, including tooling, is approximately 10–15 days. The schedule is confirmed after drawing review and agreement on the sample specification; material availability, special treatment and inspection requirements can affect the confirmed date. Confirm when the agreed schedule starts in the quotation. Sample approval and repeat-production delivery are separate milestones.

MATERIAL & FINAL CONDITION

Carbon & Alloy Steel ↗

Specify the finished fastener property class, not a strength inferred from the raw grade. Common nominal class references include 8.8, 10.9 and 12.9.

MATERIAL & FINAL CONDITION

10B21 Boron Steel ↗

10B21 is a grade designation, not a finished strength class. There is no single tensile strength that applies to every 10B21 screw.

MATERIAL & FINAL CONDITION

SUS304 & SUS316 Stainless Steel ↗

A2/A4 groups and classes such as 70 or 80 describe finished stainless fasteners under the applicable specification. SUS304 or SUS316 alone does not establish A2-70 or A4-80 performance.
