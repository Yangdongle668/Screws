Common specification questions

Can the shoulder and thread have different diameters?

Yes, the shoulder and thread are specified independently. Review the transition, available mating clearance and effective thread engagement as one geometry.

Is every shoulder screw ground?

No. The final size, form and surface requirements determine the finishing route. Review the need for grinding with treatment distortion and the final acceptance condition.

Review the fixture shoulder and axial-stack concept ↗

Choose the shoulder around its actual function

A locating shoulder, a rotating pivot and a spacing stop impose different requirements. For a pivot, examine the mating bore, running clearance and contact length. For a spacer, the shoulder length controls the clamped stack. For a locating feature, identify the datum axis and the surfaces that establish position. A close diameter tolerance alone does not resolve these different functions.

Check the axial stack before tightening

Consider a cover rotating on a shoulder screw. If the shoulder ends below the cover stack, tightening may clamp the moving part. If it projects too far, excessive axial play can remain. Show the complete stack, washers and seating face on the assembly drawing; then dimension the shoulder length against the intended clearance rather than copying the overall screw length. This is an illustrative design review, not a released customer design.

Protect the transition and final fit

The shoulder-to-thread transition needs enough clearance for the mating part to seat. Specify usable thread engagement separately from the visible threaded length and show any relief. Review straightness, runout and surface texture where these affect motion. If heat treatment or coating follows machining, establish which shoulder dimensions are accepted in the final supplied condition and whether finishing allowance is needed.

Select the route and verify the result

CNC turning is a route to review for sample quantities and complex steps. Cold heading followed by local machining can be evaluated for repeat production. Grinding is selected where size, form or surface requirements justify it, rather than assumed from the product name. CCD inspection can screen accessible profiles; datum relationships, surface texture and thread acceptance need their own suitable methods.

Review the route before fixing the quotation

For shoulder & step screws, review Cold Heading + Secondary Machining, Swiss-Type & CNC Turning, Secondary & Precision Operations against the drawing and quantity. Identify which features can be produced in the initial shape and which require a separate setup. A change to a functional shoulder, bore, drive or thread can change the proposed route as well as the inspection effort.

The drawing review should resolve shoulder diameter & length; thread & transition; precision options. Keep material condition and finishing allowance attached to those requirements. Define the acceptance condition for critical features and approve any proposed dimensional change before comparing samples with the original assembly.

From drawing to approved samples

Include mating bore details, critical shoulder dimensions, surface texture and required dimensional inspection. Identify the drawing revision, prototype quantity and expected repeat demand. Where the first part is based on an existing sample, separately confirm material and hidden features rather than inferring them from appearance.

The typical sampling lead time, including tooling, is approximately 10–15 days. The schedule is confirmed after drawing review and agreement on the sample specification; material availability, special treatment and inspection requirements can affect the confirmed date. Confirm when the agreed schedule starts in the quotation. Sample approval and repeat-production delivery are separate milestones.

How to prepare an inspection plan for custom screws ↗

An inspection plan translates a drawing into agreed checks and records. Define it during the quotation stage so acceptance is understood before production.

How is a shoulder screw different from a fully threaded bolt?

A shoulder screw has an unthreaded bearing or locating section between its head and thread. Select that shoulder for the required rotation, location or spacing function, and specify thread engagement separately. A fully threaded bolt does not provide the same smooth bearing surface.

How do I choose the shoulder length without clamping a moving part?

Review the complete axial stack, including washers and the mating seat. The shoulder must seat as intended while preserving the specified movement clearance. Show the assembly and tolerance stack on the drawing; nominal shoulder length alone cannot establish the final clearance.
