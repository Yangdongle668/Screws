Electroplated finishes

Zinc, colored or black zinc systems, bright/black nickel and zinc-nickel are discussion options. Name the actual coating specification, thickness and any friction or corrosion-test requirement. Cadmium is a restricted specialist option requiring project-specific review, not a default finish.

Zinc-flake and conversion systems

Zinc-flake systems, including Dacromet- or Geomet-type requirements, need an exact specification and applicability review. Black oxide and passivation depend on the substrate. Non-electrolytic deposition does not remove every possible hydrogen source from the full process.

Organic and vacuum coatings

Electrophoretic coating, PTFE-based coatings, powder/paint and PVD can address selected friction, appearance or surface functions. Thickness, cure temperature and thread fit need review.

High-strength fastener review

High-strength or high-hardness steels may be susceptible to hydrogen embrittlement from cleaning, pickling or plating. Baking and process controls can reduce risk but cannot guarantee its elimination. Select the coating and relief requirements under the applicable specification; neither a blanket zinc ban nor a blanket bake rule is sufficient.

Inputs and outputs for Coatings & Surface Treatments

The process review begins with Security & Tamper-Resistant Screws, Custom Flange Screws, Knurled Thumb & Adjustment Screws, Shoulder & Step Screws and the actual drawing, not a generic equipment list. Identify the incoming stock or part condition and the dimensions that must remain controlled at this stage.

Use the process topics above to resolve electroplated finishes; zinc-flake and conversion systems; organic and vacuum coatings; high-strength fastener review. Record the selected requirements in the order specification. A process name does not establish the resulting tolerance, property class or inspection coverage.

Coordinate the next operation

The next review may involve Cleaning, Locking & Assembly or CCD Vision & Dimensional Inspection. Check which surfaces are used to locate the part, whether the next operation changes dimensions and at what stage final acceptance is measured. Where a requirement cannot be checked after assembly or finishing, agree an intermediate check as well as final verification.

For samples involving tooling, the typical lead time is approximately 10–15 days, subject to drawing review and confirmation of material, finishing and inspection scope. The complete route determines the confirmed schedule; the duration is not a promise for each individual process.
