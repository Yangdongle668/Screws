Cold Heading + Secondary Machining

A formed blank provides the basic shape; selected machined features establish critical steps, faces or holes.

Why combine processes

Integrate repeated bulk geometry through forming, then machine the features that need a different tool path or control. Tooling and machining must be evaluated together.

Typical feature split

Head and rough shank by forming; shoulder finish, reliefs, flats or side holes by secondary operations. The exact split follows feasibility review.

Finish-sensitive shoulders

Heat treatment and coating can alter dimensions or surface condition. Plan precision finishing and final measurement at the appropriate stage.

Drawing requirements

Identify functional fits and datum relationships. The RFQ should distinguish critical features from dimensions with more manufacturing flexibility.

Prepare a complete specification

Use the RFQ checklist, review materials and include the required inspection and documentation.

Inputs and outputs for Cold Heading + Secondary Machining

The process review begins with Shoulder & Step Screws, Multi-Step & Multi-Shoulder Screws, Double-Ended Shoulder Screws, Custom Flange Screws and the actual drawing, not a generic equipment list. Identify the incoming stock or part condition and the dimensions that must remain controlled at this stage.

Use the process topics above to resolve why combine processes; typical feature split; finish-sensitive shoulders; drawing requirements. Record the selected requirements in the order specification. A process name does not establish the resulting tolerance, property class or inspection coverage.

Coordinate the next operation

The next review may involve Secondary & Precision Operations or Material-Specific Heat Treatment. Check which surfaces are used to locate the part, whether the next operation changes dimensions and at what stage final acceptance is measured. Where a requirement cannot be checked after assembly or finishing, agree an intermediate check as well as final verification.

For samples involving tooling, the typical lead time is approximately 10–15 days, subject to drawing review and confirmation of material, finishing and inspection scope. The complete route determines the confirmed schedule; the duration is not a promise for each individual process.
