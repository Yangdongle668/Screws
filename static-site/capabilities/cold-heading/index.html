Multi-Station Cold Heading

Wire is cut and formed through a sequence of dies. Head, flange and some shoulder features can be integrated into a repeatable production route.

Where it fits

Repeated screw geometries and production volumes that justify dedicated tooling. Head volume, material formability and extrusion limits guide the die sequence.

Operations to review

Cut-off, preforming, heading, extrusion, local reduction and pre-piercing. Deep bores, side features and complex undercuts may require later machining.

What drives the quotation

Tooling investment, batch quantity, material, head-to-shank ratios and the secondary operations that remain. Cold heading is not automatically the cheapest route at every volume.

Mechanical considerations

Cold forming can provide favorable grain flow and work hardening. Final properties still depend on material, deformation, heat treatment and verified acceptance requirements.
