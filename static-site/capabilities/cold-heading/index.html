Multi-Station Cold Heading

Wire is cut and formed through a sequence of dies. Head, flange and some shoulder features can be integrated into a repeatable production route.

Where it fits

Repeated screw geometries and production volumes that justify dedicated tooling. Head volume, material formability and extrusion limits guide the die sequence.

Operations to review

Cut-off, preforming, heading, extrusion, local reduction and pre-piercing. Deep bores, side features and complex undercuts may require later machining.

What drives the quotation

Tooling investment, batch quantity, material, head-to-shank ratios and the secondary operations that remain. Cold heading is not automatically the cheapest route at every volume.

Mechanical considerations

Cold forming can provide favorable grain flow and work hardening. Final properties still depend on material, deformation, heat treatment and verified acceptance requirements.

Inputs and outputs for Multi-Station Cold Heading

The process review begins with Shoulder & Step Screws, Custom Flange Screws, Security & Tamper-Resistant Screws, Custom Carriage Bolts and the actual drawing, not a generic equipment list. Identify the incoming stock or part condition and the dimensions that must remain controlled at this stage.

Use the process topics above to resolve where it fits; operations to review; what drives the quotation; mechanical considerations. Record the selected requirements in the order specification. A process name does not establish the resulting tolerance, property class or inspection coverage.

Coordinate the next operation

The next review may involve Cold Heading + Secondary Machining or Thread Rolling, Cutting & Tapping. Check which surfaces are used to locate the part, whether the next operation changes dimensions and at what stage final acceptance is measured. Where a requirement cannot be checked after assembly or finishing, agree an intermediate check as well as final verification.

For samples involving tooling, the typical lead time is approximately 10–15 days, subject to drawing review and confirmation of material, finishing and inspection scope. The complete route determines the confirmed schedule; the duration is not a promise for each individual process.
