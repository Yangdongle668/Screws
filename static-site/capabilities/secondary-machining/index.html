Secondary & Precision Operations

Local holes, flats, grooves and finished diameters often create the functional difference in a non-standard fastener.

Drilling, reaming and milling

Axial bores, radial holes, pin holes, flats, squares, drive recesses and local slots. Define position, edge breaks, hole exits and access.

Knurling, grooves and tip work

Straight or diamond knurls, circlip grooves, thread reliefs, oil grooves, tip cuts and controlled end shapes. Some features can be formed; others are machined.

Eccentric, angled and press operations

Offset turning, angled faces, undercuts, clinching and controlled crimping require an explicit setup and inspection plan.

Grinding and specialist routes

External, centerless or thread grinding may suit specific size, surface and form requirements. A g6 or h7 shaft tolerance does not mandate centerless grinding in every case. Wire EDM and laser operations are reviewed only where geometry and economics justify them.

Inputs and outputs for Secondary & Precision Operations

The process review begins with Hollow & Through-Hole Screws, Knurled Thumb & Adjustment Screws, Eccentric & Angled Head Screws, Cross-Drilled & Head-Hole Screws and the actual drawing, not a generic equipment list. Identify the incoming stock or part condition and the dimensions that must remain controlled at this stage.

Use the process topics above to resolve drilling, reaming and milling; knurling, grooves and tip work; eccentric, angled and press operations; grinding and specialist routes. Record the selected requirements in the order specification. A process name does not establish the resulting tolerance, property class or inspection coverage.

Coordinate the next operation

The next review may involve Cleaning, Locking & Assembly or CCD Vision & Dimensional Inspection. Check which surfaces are used to locate the part, whether the next operation changes dimensions and at what stage final acceptance is measured. Where a requirement cannot be checked after assembly or finishing, agree an intermediate check as well as final verification.

For samples involving tooling, the typical lead time is approximately 10–15 days, subject to drawing review and confirmation of material, finishing and inspection scope. The complete route determines the confirmed schedule; the duration is not a promise for each individual process.
