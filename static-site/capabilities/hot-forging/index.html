Hot Heading & Forging

Heated stock can be formed into larger or heavier geometries before finishing operations define the final interfaces.

Where it fits

Large heads, eye forms and substantial sections may suit hot forging. There is no universal M24 changeover; material, equipment and shape determine the practical route.

Typical follow-up work

Trimming, scale removal, machining of seating faces or holes, threading and the specified heat treatment.

Load-bearing features

Radii, section transitions and material condition must be considered with the intended load. Lifting-related designs need an approved specification and appropriate verification.

Quotation inputs

Include stock grade, geometry, quantity, mechanical requirements and the scope of any required testing and documentation.

Prepare a complete specification

Use the RFQ checklist, review materials and include the required inspection and documentation.

Inputs and outputs for Hot Heading & Forging

The process review begins with Custom Eye & Articulating Bolts, Custom Lifting Eye Bolt Designs, Custom Square Head Bolts and the actual drawing, not a generic equipment list. Identify the incoming stock or part condition and the dimensions that must remain controlled at this stage.

Use the process topics above to resolve where it fits; typical follow-up work; load-bearing features; quotation inputs. Record the selected requirements in the order specification. A process name does not establish the resulting tolerance, property class or inspection coverage.

Coordinate the next operation

The next review may involve Material-Specific Heat Treatment or Thread Rolling, Cutting & Tapping. Check which surfaces are used to locate the part, whether the next operation changes dimensions and at what stage final acceptance is measured. Where a requirement cannot be checked after assembly or finishing, agree an intermediate check as well as final verification.

For samples involving tooling, the typical lead time is approximately 10–15 days, subject to drawing review and confirmation of material, finishing and inspection scope. The complete route determines the confirmed schedule; the duration is not a promise for each individual process.
