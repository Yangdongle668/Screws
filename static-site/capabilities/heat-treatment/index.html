Material-Specific Heat Treatment

Heat-treatment selection follows the alloy, required condition and applicable acceptance specification. One sequence cannot be applied to every screw.

Quench and temper

Suitable carbon and alloy steels can be processed to specified mechanical requirements. Property classes such as 8.8, 10.9 and 12.9 require an applicable specification and verification; treatment names alone do not establish the class.

Case hardening and boron steel

Carburizing and hardening can produce a hardened surface with a different core condition. A grade such as 10B21 does not by itself prescribe carburizing: specify core properties, case requirements and application.

Stainless and titanium distinctions

304/316 austenitic stainless steels are not precipitation-hardening grades. Solution annealing serves a different purpose from aging 17-4PH. Titanium and other alloys need grade-specific treatment; do not transfer a stainless cycle to them.

Stress relief and stability

Stress-relief or specialist cryogenic treatments are selected only where justified by material condition and dimensional needs. State the required final condition and review distortion, corrosion and finish compatibility.
