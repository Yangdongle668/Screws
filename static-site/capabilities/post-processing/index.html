Cleaning, Locking & Assembly

Cleanliness, burr control and pre-applied features are part of the finished-part specification, not an afterthought.

Cleaning and burr control

Degreasing, ultrasonic or suitable solvent cleaning, deburring, vibratory finishing and polishing are selected for the material and feature access. Define internal-bore and thread burr limits explicitly.

Technical cleanliness

If VDA 19 or ISO 16232 is specified, agree the applicable method, extraction, particle limits and reporting. Mentioning the standard alone does not define the acceptance level.

Pre-applied locking systems

Nylon patches and adhesive-based locking systems have different mechanisms. Specify the system, coverage, assembly conditions, reuse needs and required torque behavior.

Pre-assembly and sorting

Washers, O-rings, PTFE washers or springs may be included by drawing. Confirm sequence, retention and compatibility with heat/coating operations. Hydrogen-relief baking is applied where required by the selected material and finish specification.

Inputs and outputs for Cleaning, Locking & Assembly

The process review begins with Hollow & Through-Hole Screws, Captive Screws, Custom Flange Screws, Modified-Tip Self-Tapping Screws and the actual drawing, not a generic equipment list. Identify the incoming stock or part condition and the dimensions that must remain controlled at this stage.

Use the process topics above to resolve cleaning and burr control; technical cleanliness; pre-applied locking systems; pre-assembly and sorting. Record the selected requirements in the order specification. A process name does not establish the resulting tolerance, property class or inspection coverage.

Coordinate the next operation

The next review may involve CCD Vision & Dimensional Inspection or Coatings & Surface Treatments. Check which surfaces are used to locate the part, whether the next operation changes dimensions and at what stage final acceptance is measured. Where a requirement cannot be checked after assembly or finishing, agree an intermediate check as well as final verification.

For samples involving tooling, the typical lead time is approximately 10–15 days, subject to drawing review and confirmation of material, finishing and inspection scope. The complete route determines the confirmed schedule; the duration is not a promise for each individual process.
