Choosing material for a custom metal screw

Material selection begins with the service environment and mechanical requirements. The best specification balances function, manufacturability, availability and verification.

By Theo Wan • ZUFEK Technical Editorial • Published October 29, 2026

RELATED PRODUCTCustom Metal Screws ↗

Describe the environment first

State whether the part will encounter moisture, chemicals, heat or outdoor exposure. Include contact with dissimilar metals and any cleaning process. Avoid choosing a grade based on appearance alone.

Define the mechanical requirements

Provide the applicable strength, hardness or other performance requirements where relevant. Different grades and processing conditions can behave differently; a material family alone is not a complete mechanical specification.

Working on this type of part? Explore our custom metal screws and include the functional requirements in your RFQ.

Treat finish as part of the system

Coatings may influence dimensions, friction and assembly behavior. Identify the required finish and the surfaces on which it matters. Allowances should be reviewed against the drawing and actual process.

Confirm documentation needs

Tell the supplier whether you require material documentation, inspection records or other supporting evidence. Confirm availability and acceptance requirements before placing an order.

Ready to discuss your part?

Use the RFQ preparation checklist, then submit your drawing for review. For acceptance requirements, see quality planning.

General guidance only. Material properties, tolerances, standards and manufacturing feasibility must be verified for the actual specification. This article does not establish a product performance guarantee.
