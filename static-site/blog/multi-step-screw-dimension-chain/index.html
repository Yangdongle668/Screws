Multi-step screws: controlling the axial dimension chain

Several shoulders can locate several components. A clear dimension scheme explains which axial distances control the assembly and which are consequences of other dimensions.

By Theo Wan • ZUFEK Technical Editorial • Published October 29, 2026

RELATED PRODUCTMulti-Step & Multi-Shoulder Screws ↗

Map the shoulder to each mating component

Identify which face supports each component in the stack. Include the mating parts or a simplified section showing their position. This helps separate functional locating shoulders from machining transitions that do not establish an assembly position.

Choose a consistent reference scheme

Baseline dimensions and chained dimensions communicate different relationships. The responsible designer should select the scheme that matches the required function and tolerance analysis. Avoid adding a closed chain of conflicting controlled dimensions simply to fill every gap on the drawing.

Working on this type of part? Explore our multi-step & multi-shoulder screws and include the functional requirements in your RFQ.

Review transitions and tool access

Small reliefs, corner radii and edge breaks can affect how a component seats against a shoulder. Show the mating corner conditions and identify features that must remain clear. Secondary machining or finishing needs to preserve the approved seating geometry.

Inspect the relevant relationships

A collection of individual lengths does not always establish the functional stack. Agree measurement references and checks for the distances that matter. Include final coating and treatment conditions when they can affect the supplied diameters or seating surfaces.

Specification example

For two bearings separated by a shoulder, identify the bearing seating faces and the required spacing between them. This is more useful than applying the same tight tolerance to every unrelated axial dimension.

RFQ checklist

  • Mating stack or section view
  • Functional locating shoulders
  • Datum-based axial dimensions
  • Corner and relief requirements
  • Final-condition inspection references
