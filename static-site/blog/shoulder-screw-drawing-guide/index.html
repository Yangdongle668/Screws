A practical guide to shoulder screw drawings

The shoulder, thread and head work together as one geometry. A clear drawing explains not only the dimensions, but also which surfaces locate, rotate or clamp in your assembly.

Define the shoulder

Dimension shoulder diameter and shoulder length independently from thread length. Explain whether the shoulder serves as a locating surface, bearing surface or spacer. The functional role helps identify which features need closer review.

Show how the thread ends

State thread designation and length, and show any relief or transition at the shoulder. Check how the screw seats in the mating assembly. Do not assume the end of the usable thread is identical to the end of the visible thread form.

Working on this type of part? Explore our shoulder screws and include the functional requirements in your RFQ.

Review head access and seating

Include head diameter, height and drive configuration. Consider the tool clearance and seating surface in the assembly. A head that fits the drawing may still be inaccessible once neighboring parts are installed.

Communicate functional tolerances

Mark the mating bore relationship and any surface finish requirement that affects motion or fit. Tolerances should be established by the application engineer and confirmed during manufacturing review.

Ready to discuss your part?

Use the RFQ preparation checklist, then submit your drawing for review. For acceptance requirements, see quality planning.

General guidance only. Material properties, tolerances, standards and manufacturing feasibility must be verified for the actual specification. This article does not establish a product performance guarantee.
