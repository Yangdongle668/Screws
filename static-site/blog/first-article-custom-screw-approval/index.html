First-article approval for custom screws: a practical handoff

A sample is most useful when its identity, revision and acceptance scope are clear. First-article planning connects the approved drawing with the evidence needed before the next production decision.

By Theo Wan • ZUFEK Technical Editorial • Published October 29, 2026

RELATED PRODUCTCustom Metal Screws ↗

Link the sample to a controlled specification

Record drawing number, revision, material condition and finish. Identify any agreed deviations separately. A loose sample with no revision history can create confusion if it is later used as a master for a repeat order or a manufacturing change.

Define the evidence before making parts

Agree which dimensions, material documents, process records and functional checks are required. The sample plan should follow the actual risk and acceptance requirements. Waiting until parts arrive to request additional tests can delay approval or require another manufacturing batch.

Working on this type of part? Explore our custom metal screws and include the functional requirements in your RFQ.

Separate visual and functional approval

Appearance, tool fit and trial assembly can reveal useful information, but they do not replace all drawing checks. Record what each review establishes and who accepts it. If a mating fixture is used, identify its revision and relevant condition as part of the approval record.

Close the feedback loop

Document changes arising from the review and decide whether they require a new drawing revision or another sample. Preserve an approved reference only with a clear record of what it controls. Production release should refer to the final agreed specification rather than an informal message chain.

Specification example

If a trial assembly leads to a longer shoulder, revise the drawing and identify the replacement sample. Keeping the original sample as an unexplained master can reintroduce the superseded dimension on a later order.

RFQ checklist

  • Drawing revision and sample identity
  • Required dimensional report
  • Material and process evidence
  • Defined assembly or appearance checks
  • Recorded approval and change disposition
Ready to discuss your part?

Use the RFQ preparation checklist, then submit your drawing for review. For acceptance requirements, see quality planning.
