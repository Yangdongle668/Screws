What to include in a custom screw RFQ

A useful quotation starts with a clear specification. A drawing is the best starting point, but a sample, sketch or assembly description can also help define the next questions.

By Theo Wan • ZUFEK Technical Editorial • Published October 29, 2026

Start with the drawing and revision

Include the drawing number, revision, units and quantity. Show overall length, thread specification, head dimensions and any shoulders. If a requirement is still open, label it as open rather than leaving it ambiguous.

Identify the features that matter

Mark dimensions that affect fit or function. Explain the mating part, assembly access and service conditions. A tolerance on every surface may create unnecessary cost; let functional needs guide the discussion.

Working on this type of part? Explore our custom metal screws and include the functional requirements in your RFQ.

Specify material and finish separately

State a material grade if it is known. Identify any coating, appearance or corrosion requirements separately. A material family or a finish name alone may not be enough to define an acceptance requirement.

Include quantity and delivery context

Provide prototype quantity, initial order quantity and expected repeat demand if available. Include the destination and requested date. Lead time and feasibility need confirmation after technical review.

Your RFQ checklist

Drawing or sketch • revision and units • material • finish • critical features • quantity • inspection documents • destination. Downloading a checklist is optional: you can paste these details directly into the quotation form.

Ready to discuss your part?

Use the RFQ preparation checklist, then submit your drawing for review. For acceptance requirements, see quality planning.

General guidance only. Material properties, tolerances, standards and manufacturing feasibility must be verified for the actual specification. This article does not establish a product performance guarantee.
