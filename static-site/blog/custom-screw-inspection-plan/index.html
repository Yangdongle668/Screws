How to prepare an inspection plan for custom screws

An inspection plan translates a drawing into agreed checks and records. Define it during the quotation stage so acceptance is understood before production.

By Theo Wan • ZUFEK Technical Editorial • Published October 29, 2026

Use the current drawing

Link the inspection plan to the drawing number and revision. Resolve unclear notes and conflicting requirements before the part is released.

Identify critical characteristics

List the dimensions, thread requirements, material and finish characteristics to be verified. Distinguish dimensional checks from functional assembly checks.

Working on this type of part? Explore our shoulder & step screws and include the functional requirements in your RFQ.

Agree on methods and records

Discuss measurement methods, sampling requirements and required reporting. Equipment suitability and acceptance criteria should be agreed for the actual features involved.

Plan sample approval

Where required, confirm how samples will be identified, reviewed and approved. Record approved changes so repeat orders refer to the same specification.

Build a characteristic-level matrix

For every agreed critical characteristic, list drawing reference, requirement, method, inspection frequency and retained record. Separate an external dimension, a thread fit, a material property and a coating characteristic. Naming a machine does not define which of these it verifies. Set the coverage before the order so purchasing and quality use the same acceptance basis.

Plan inspection at the stage that provides evidence

An internal edge may become less accessible after assembly, while a fit may need acceptance after finishing. Agree any intermediate check as well as final checks. Destructive testing cannot inspect every supplied part in the same manner as an external dimensional screen; define its sample and traceability separately.

Control revisions and nonconforming results

Identify the drawing and inspection-program revision with the lot. Define how rejected pieces are segregated and who decides disposition. When a dimension or surface process changes, review the affected inspection criteria rather than assuming the old program remains suitable. Keep sample approval decisions available for repeat orders.

Ready to discuss your part?

Use the RFQ preparation checklist, then submit your drawing for review. For acceptance requirements, see quality planning.

General guidance only. Material properties, tolerances, standards and manufacturing feasibility must be verified for the actual specification. This article does not establish a product performance guarantee.
