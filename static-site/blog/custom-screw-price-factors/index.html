What affects the price of a non-standard screw?

A custom screw quotation reflects the complete specification and order conditions. Understanding the cost drivers can make a design review more productive.

By Theo Wan • ZUFEK Technical Editorial • Published October 29, 2026
IN THIS GUIDEGeometry and manufacturing routeTolerance and inspectionMaterial, finish and order volumeDiscuss changes before revising
RELATED PRODUCTSpecial Head Screws ↗

Geometry and manufacturing route

Head shape, length ratios, steps, internal features and thread configuration affect the manufacturing options. Feasibility needs review against the actual drawing and available processes.

Tolerance and inspection

Tighter requirements can change processing and measurement effort. Identify truly critical dimensions so the review focuses on the features that drive function.

Working on this type of part? Explore our special head screws and include the functional requirements in your RFQ.

Material, finish and order volume

Material grade, secondary operations, finish requirements and batch size affect the quotation. Prototype and repeat production quantities may have different cost structures.

Discuss changes before revising

Ask which drawing features influence cost. Any proposed change should be reviewed and approved by the responsible engineer before manufacturing. A lower quoted price is only useful if the part still meets the application requirements.

Ready to discuss your part?

Use the RFQ preparation checklist, then submit your drawing for review. For acceptance requirements, see quality planning.

General guidance only. Material properties, tolerances, standards and manufacturing feasibility must be verified for the actual specification. This article does not establish a product performance guarantee.
