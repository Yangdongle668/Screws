Eye bolts for linkages and lifting: different specification needs

An eye-shaped end can belong to a linkage, a tensioning device or a lifting component. Similar appearance does not establish the same design requirements or approved use.

By Theo Wan • ZUFEK Technical Editorial • Published October 29, 2026

Define the intended function first

State how the component connects and how force enters the eye. Include the pin, supporting parts and expected movement. A flat drilled lug, a fork and a closed forged ring create different interfaces even when each is casually called an eye bolt.

Show the articulation and load direction

Provide the range of movement and surrounding clearances. Identify whether the connection sees aligned tension, angular loading or changing directions. These details help the responsible engineer assess the load path and select the dimensions that require control.

Working on this type of part? Explore our custom eye & articulating bolts and include the functional requirements in your RFQ.

Treat lifting requirements explicitly

A custom ring geometry is not a working-load rating. Lifting-related use requires an approved design basis, suitable material condition and the specified verification and documentation. Do not infer a rating from dimensions, appearance or similarity to an unrelated catalog component.

Prepare a complete manufacturing review

Dimension the eye, shank transitions, thread and seating faces. State the required properties and acceptance checks. The forming and machining sequence should be considered with the function, rather than selecting a production method before the application requirements are understood.

Specification example

For a pivoting fixture connection, provide the mating clevis and pin. For a lifting requirement, include the approved lifting specification instead of using the fixture drawing as a substitute.

RFQ checklist

  • Linkage or lifting use
  • Mating pin and movement envelope
  • Load directions and design requirements
  • Material and final condition
  • Required verification and records
Ready to discuss your part?

Use the RFQ preparation checklist, then submit your drawing for review. For acceptance requirements, see quality planning.
