Shoulder fits, thread classes and when grinding matters

A shaft fit and a thread tolerance class control different interfaces. Write the required function clearly before deciding how the surfaces must be finished.

By Theo Wan • ZUFEK Technical Editorial • Published October 29, 2026

Separate the plain shoulder and the thread

A shaft tolerance such as g6 or h7 describes a plain cylindrical feature. A designation such as 6g or 6H belongs to a metric thread tolerance system. Case and order matter; these are not interchangeable callouts.

Identify the mating relationship

Provide the mating bore, shoulder length, datums and surface texture. Clearance, locating behavior and rotation depend on both components and their operating conditions.

Working on this type of part? Explore our shoulder & step screws and include the functional requirements in your RFQ.

Specify grinding only with a reason

Grinding can be useful for size, form and surface finish, especially after a treatment that changes dimensions. A tolerance designation alone does not prove that centerless grinding is the only acceptable route.

Inspect after the relevant finishing stage

Review coating allowance and final condition. Agree measurement methods for size, form and datum relationships, together with thread gauging and the required inspection records.

Ready to discuss your part?

Use the RFQ preparation checklist, then submit your drawing for review. For acceptance requirements, see quality planning.
