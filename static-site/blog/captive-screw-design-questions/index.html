Five questions before specifying a captive screw

A captive screw is part of a retention system. Review the panel, retaining feature and mating thread together before finalizing the screw drawing.

By Theo Wan • ZUFEK Technical Editorial • Published October 29, 2026

What retains the screw?

Describe the intended retaining component or geometry. Provide an assembly view and panel details. The screw alone does not establish whether the completed assembly will remain captive.

How much movement is needed?

Show required travel, open position and thread engagement. Explain how the operator installs and releases the fastener, including available access.

Working on this type of part? Explore our captive screws and include the functional requirements in your RFQ.

What happens during service?

Describe repeated access, vibration, cleaning and exposure conditions. These inputs inform the material, finish and design discussion; they do not replace assembly testing.

How will the assembly be verified?

Define retention and functional acceptance criteria with your engineering team. A dimensional inspection and an assembly test answer different questions.

What should accompany the RFQ?

Include the screw drawing, mating assembly, quantity and any sample photos. Unresolved retention details can be reviewed before a final quotation is issued.

Ready to discuss your part?

Use the RFQ preparation checklist, then submit your drawing for review. For acceptance requirements, see quality planning.

General guidance only. Material properties, tolerances, standards and manufacturing feasibility must be verified for the actual specification. This article does not establish a product performance guarantee.
