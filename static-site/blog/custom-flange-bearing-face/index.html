Designing the bearing face of a custom flange screw

A flange is more than a larger circle under the head. Its bearing face, stiffness and clearance must match the component being clamped.

By Theo Wan • ZUFEK Technical Editorial • Published October 29, 2026

Start with the mating surface

Identify the material beneath the flange and the available contact area. A soft panel, coated bracket and rigid machined seat respond differently. Show holes, slots and nearby edges so the bearing footprint can be reviewed against the real supporting surface.

Dimension the flange as a functional feature

Specify diameter, thickness and the transition beneath the head. Include flatness or surface requirements when they affect contact. Thin flanges and large overhangs should not be assumed to distribute load uniformly; their behavior needs review with the intended connection and material condition.

Working on this type of part? Explore our custom flange screws and include the functional requirements in your RFQ.

Choose the face condition deliberately

Plain, textured and serrated bearing faces are not interchangeable. A serration may change the contact behavior and affect a coating or soft surface. Explain whether the priority is appearance, resistance to movement, contact pressure or another function, then agree the relevant acceptance checks.

Include the finish and tightening conditions

Coating thickness and friction can influence the assembled joint. Treat the flange face and threads as specified surfaces, not simply parts of an overall color requirement. Any comparison with a washer assembly should consider the complete joint rather than the number of handled components.

Specification example

When replacing a loose washer with an integral flange, provide both assembly drawings. The review should compare the supporting footprint and seating condition before treating the two arrangements as equivalent.

RFQ checklist

  • Mating material and hole shape
  • Flange diameter and thickness
  • Bearing-face condition
  • Clearance and finish requirements
  • Assembly acceptance criteria

Use the RFQ preparation checklist, then submit your drawing for review. For acceptance requirements, see quality planning.
