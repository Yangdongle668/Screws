T-slot bolt fit: what to measure before ordering

A T-head must enter, turn or slide inside the intended slot while retaining the required contact. The slot cross-section is therefore an essential part of the fastener RFQ.

By Theo Wan • ZUFEK Technical Editorial • Published October 29, 2026

RELATED PRODUCTCustom T-Slot Bolts ↗

Provide the complete slot section

Measure the opening, internal width, depth and any corner radii. Explain which dimensions are controlled and whether the profile varies along the assembly. A nominal extrusion family or a top-view photograph may leave important internal clearances undefined.

Explain the insertion method

Some heads enter from a slot end; others must pass through the opening and rotate. These are different geometric requirements. Show the intended assembly sequence and any end stops or surrounding parts that prevent the movement assumed during installation.

Working on this type of part? Explore our custom t-slot bolts and include the functional requirements in your RFQ.

Review contact and anti-rotation

Head thickness, bearing faces and neck geometry influence how the bolt seats in the slot. Specify the required clearances and contact surfaces. A head that can be inserted is not automatically suitable for the required clamping load or resistance to rotation.

Check the finished condition

Coating and edge finishing can alter the available clearance. Agree the final head envelope and inspect the relevant features after finishing. A trial fit in an approved mating section may be useful, but it should support clearly documented dimensional and functional requirements.

Specification example

For a bolt that must rotate inside an aluminum profile, provide the rotation envelope as well as the installed position. This identifies interference that a single assembled view may hide.

RFQ checklist

  • Slot cross-section and radii
  • Insertion and rotation sequence
  • Head and neck clearances
  • Finished coating allowance
  • Agreed fit and clamping checks
Ready to discuss your part?

Use the RFQ preparation checklist, then submit your drawing for review. For acceptance requirements, see quality planning.
