Cone, flat, dog or cup: choosing a set-screw tip

The end of a set screw defines how it contacts the mating component. Choose the tip around the required contact and permitted surface change, not only the screw diameter.

By Theo Wan • ZUFEK Technical Editorial • Published October 29, 2026

Identify what the tip contacts

Show the shaft, flat, groove or hole beneath the screw. A tip contacting a curved surface behaves differently from one engaging a prepared recess. Explain whether the connection is intended to clamp, locate, stop travel or provide an anti-rotation feature.

Compare the actual end geometries

Cone, flat, cylindrical dog and cup points are different contact forms. Dimension the functional features of the selected end and its relationship to the thread. Avoid treating a generic tip name as permission for any manufacturer-specific interpretation of the shape.

Working on this type of part? Explore our custom set screws & grub screws and include the functional requirements in your RFQ.

Review marking and repeated adjustment

State whether the mating surface may be indented or scratched and whether the screw will be repositioned. Surface condition can become important when a setting is changed repeatedly. Tip material condition, finish and the mating material should be reviewed as part of the same contact system.

Define engagement and acceptance

Thread engagement, drive access and installation requirements still matter on a headless part. Agree a dimensional check for the tip and any assembly test needed for the intended function. A correct tip outline alone does not establish a holding-force or torque rating.

Specification example

If a dog point enters a prepared hole, supply that hole geometry with the screw drawing. The cylindrical extension and the mating hole should be reviewed together rather than specified independently.

RFQ checklist

  • Mating surface or recess
  • Tip form and controlled dimensions
  • Allowed surface marking
  • Thread engagement and drive
  • Functional acceptance requirement
