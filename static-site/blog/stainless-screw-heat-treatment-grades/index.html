304, 316 and 17-4PH: different heat-treatment questions

Stainless steel describes several material families. Specify the grade and final condition rather than writing one generic heat-treatment instruction.

By Theo Wan • ZUFEK Technical Editorial • Published October 29, 2026

Distinguish austenitic from precipitation-hardening grades

304 and 316 are austenitic stainless steels and are not hardened by the precipitation-aging treatment used for 17-4PH. They can strengthen through cold work, which must be considered separately from heat treatment.

State the purpose of solution treatment

Solution annealing and stress-related treatments have material-specific purposes and can affect corrosion behavior and dimensional condition. They should not be treated as a universal hardening cycle.

Define the final condition of 17-4PH

A precipitation-hardening grade requires an explicitly selected condition and corresponding property requirements. Do not assume that any aging cycle is interchangeable with another.

Coordinate finish and verification

Passivation is a surface treatment, not the same operation as solution treatment or precipitation hardening. State material evidence, final properties, critical dimensions and requested inspection records.
