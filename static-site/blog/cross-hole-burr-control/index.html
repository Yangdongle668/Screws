Cross holes in screws: planning burr control and inspection

A drilled cross hole creates entry and exit edges, and may intersect another feature. The RFQ should define those edges as carefully as the hole location.

By Theo Wan • ZUFEK Technical Editorial • Published October 29, 2026

Show every intersection

Include sections where the hole crosses a bore, groove or thread. State the functional purpose of the passage and which surfaces the pin, wire or fluid contacts. An external view may hide the most difficult burr-removal location inside the component.

Specify edge condition rather than a vague note

An instruction to remove burrs needs an acceptance basis. Identify permitted edge breaks and surfaces that must retain their geometry. Excessive edge removal can change a small locating feature, while an inaccessible residual burr can interfere with the assembly or passage.

Working on this type of part? Explore our cross-drilled & head-hole screws and include the functional requirements in your RFQ.

Review the process with the feature access

Drilling order, fixturing and subsequent finishing influence access to the edges. Agree how the proposed route handles the exit and any internal intersection. A later cleaning operation removes loose contamination but should not be assumed to eliminate attached machining burrs.

Make the inspection practical

Define where visual, optical or other checks are needed and how the difficult area will be evaluated. A pin-fit test may check one function without proving all edges meet the required condition. Keep the dimensional, functional and cleanliness requirements distinct in the inspection plan.

Specification example

For a transverse pin hole through a hollow screw, show the internal intersection and describe how the pin is inserted. This gives the manufacturer a concrete edge condition to review before quoting.

RFQ checklist

  • Hole position and direction
  • Internal intersections
  • Permitted edge breaks
  • Deburring access and sequence
  • Agreed inspection method

Use the RFQ preparation checklist, then submit your drawing for review. For acceptance requirements, see quality planning.
