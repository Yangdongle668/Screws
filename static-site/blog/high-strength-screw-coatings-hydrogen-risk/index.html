High-strength screw coatings and hydrogen-risk review

A high-strength steel fastener needs a coating decision that considers both corrosion and hydrogen exposure throughout processing.

By Theo Wan • ZUFEK Technical Editorial • Published October 29, 2026

Identify the susceptible condition

Strength, hardness, material condition and tensile stress influence susceptibility. Cleaning, pickling and electroplating can be hydrogen sources. Property class alone is not a complete risk assessment.

Define a coating system

Specify the substrate, process, coating thickness, friction and acceptance requirements. Zinc-flake deposition can avoid electrolytic deposition, but pretreatment and other steps must still be reviewed.

Working on this type of part? Explore our shoulder & step screws and include the functional requirements in your RFQ.

Understand the limit of baking

Hydrogen-relief baking can reduce risk under a controlled specification. It is not a guarantee that every fastener is free from embrittlement. Define any required process controls and verification with the responsible technical team.

Write a useful RFQ

Include grade, final properties, coating specification, functional fits, documentation and application requirements. Avoid a blanket rule that every zinc finish is forbidden or that every coating requires the same baking cycle.

Map potential exposure through the full route

Review cleaning and pretreatment alongside deposition. A non-electrolytic coating does not remove every possible hydrogen source elsewhere in the sequence. Relate the process to the specified material condition and application stress. Avoid treating either a coating trade name or a property-class label as a complete risk assessment.

Define evidence before the order

The responsible technical review should establish the applicable process controls, any relief requirement, verification and records. A generic statement that parts were baked omits the conditions that made the process applicable. Risk reduction is not a guarantee of elimination; decide the acceptance scope under the actual specification rather than substituting a blanket zinc ban or blanket bake instruction.

Keep corrosion, friction and fit in the same review

Changing a coating to address one concern can alter thread allowance, friction or the bearing surface. Reassess the assembly and dimensional requirements when the surface system changes. A corrosion test result under stated conditions does not prove field life or the absence of embrittlement. These are different acceptance questions.

Ready to discuss your part?

Use the RFQ preparation checklist, then submit your drawing for review. For acceptance requirements, see quality planning.

General guidance only. Material properties, tolerances, standards and manufacturing feasibility must be verified for the actual specification. This article does not establish a product performance guarantee.
