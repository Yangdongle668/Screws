Coating allowance for threads and precision shoulders

A coating belongs in the dimensional plan. The final fit must be evaluated in the condition that the customer receives, not only before surface treatment.

By Theo Wan • ZUFEK Technical Editorial • Published October 29, 2026
IN THIS GUIDEIdentify the surfaces that control fitSpecify the actual coating systemResolve the inspection stageInclude local finish restrictions
RELATED PRODUCTShoulder & Step Screws ↗

Identify the surfaces that control fit

Mark the shoulder, thread, bearing face and any bore that interfaces with another component. Explain whether coating is required, permitted or excluded on each functional area. An all-over finish note may conflict with a fit that the drawing defines without considering the surface system.

Specify the actual coating system

Name the relevant finish requirement and thickness controls rather than using color alone. Different systems can have different dimensional and friction effects. The manufacturer needs the agreed system to propose appropriate preparation and finishing allowances for the specific geometry.

Working on this type of part? Explore our shoulder & step screws and include the functional requirements in your RFQ.

Resolve the inspection stage

State whether dimensions refer to the pre-coating or final condition. If both stages matter, identify their separate limits and checks. Do not assume that a shoulder accepted before finishing or a thread accepted before coating will automatically meet the final assembly requirement.

Include local finish restrictions

Masking, selective treatment or post-finish operations may be relevant, but must be reviewed for feasibility and function. Agree any permitted bare surfaces and protection requirements. A route change intended to rescue a fit should not silently change the approved surface condition.

Specification example

For a locating shoulder with a coated thread, distinguish the two surface requirements on the drawing. This allows the manufacturer to review whether one uniform treatment is appropriate.

RFQ checklist

  • Functional coated surfaces
  • Finish system and thickness requirements
  • Pre-finish versus final dimensions
  • Selective coating restrictions
  • Final thread and shoulder checks
Ready to discuss your part?

Use the RFQ preparation checklist, then submit your drawing for review. For acceptance requirements, see quality planning.
