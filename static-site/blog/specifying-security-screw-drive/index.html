How to specify a tamper-resistant screw drive

A security-drive name is a starting point, not a complete interface specification. The recess, matching tool and available assembly access must work together.

By Theo Wan • ZUFEK Technical Editorial • Published October 29, 2026

Identify the exact interface

Pin-in six-lobe, five-lobe, Y-type, triangle and two-hole spanner designs are different interfaces. Even visually similar recesses can have incompatible profiles. Supply a controlled drive drawing, recognized interface reference or approved matching tool rather than asking a manufacturer to copy a small photograph.

Define the head and tool access

Head diameter, height and surrounding enclosure affect whether the intended tool can reach the recess. Show the assembled condition, including any counterbore or nearby wall. A usable loose screw can become difficult to service after it is installed inside the product.

Working on this type of part? Explore our security & tamper-resistant screws and include the functional requirements in your RFQ.

Review pin and recess features

For a center-pin drive, include the pin geometry, recess depth and relevant edge condition. Forming and secondary machining offer different ways to create these details. Inspect the functional tool engagement as well as the external head dimensions; a recess can look correct while fitting poorly.

Separate access resistance from joint performance

A special drive can discourage casual removal, but it does not make a joint impossible to open. Material, thread engagement and the tightening method still determine the mechanical connection. Define service access, installation torque requirements and the intended tool-control arrangement independently.

Specification example

For an enclosure fastener, supply the enclosure recess and service-tool outline alongside the screw drawing. This allows the quotation review to consider drive engagement and access together before tooling is selected.

RFQ checklist

  • Drive drawing or approved tool
  • Head envelope and access clearance
  • Pin and recess dimensions
  • Material, finish and quantity
  • Functional tool-fit acceptance
