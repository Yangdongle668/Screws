Cold heading or CNC turning: choosing a custom screw route

Choose the manufacturing route around geometry, quantity, material and required condition. A lower cycle cost does not always offset tooling or secondary work.

By Theo Wan • ZUFEK Technical Editorial • Published October 29, 2026

Start with the repeat volume

Cold heading can be economical for repeated geometries at a suitable volume, while CNC turning can offer flexible setup for prototypes and varying features. The actual break-even point needs a quotation for the drawing, not a universal quantity rule.

Separate bulk shape from functional detail

A head and rough shoulder may suit forming, while a bore, flat or critical shoulder needs machining. A hybrid route should be costed as a complete sequence rather than comparing only the first operation.

Working on this type of part? Explore our custom metal screws and include the functional requirements in your RFQ.

Check the material and acceptance requirements

Formability, stock condition and required final properties influence both routes. Machining is not universally weaker than cold heading: material, geometry, heat treatment and load case determine performance.

Review the complete route

Include thread generation, any treatment, precision finishing, coating, cleaning and inspection. Specify which dimensions are functional so the proposed process protects the relevant datums and fits.

Apply the guidance to the assembly

Start with the interface a standard screw cannot satisfy. Use this route when the requirement combines features or does not fit a single product family. Identify the reason for customization: inaccessible drive, unusual bearing face, mixed threads, internal passage or several locating steps. Separate essential interfaces from dimensions that can be discussed during manufacturability review.

Compare tooling and the cost of the approved finished part

Ask for tooling cost and recurring part cost separately at the sample quantity and intended repeat quantity. Include the same material condition, coating and inspection scope in both proposals. A headed blank that still needs boring, turning and precision finishing cannot be compared directly with a fully finished CNC quotation. Identify which tooling can remain usable if a critical dimension changes after sample evaluation.

Ready to discuss your part?

Use the RFQ preparation checklist, then submit your drawing for review. For acceptance requirements, see quality planning.

General guidance only. Material properties, tolerances, standards and manufacturing feasibility must be verified for the actual specification. This article does not establish a product performance guarantee.
