Custom carriage bolts: specifying the square neck

The square neck is the functional anti-rotation feature of a carriage bolt. It should be matched to the mating hole and supporting material rather than treated as a cosmetic detail.

By Theo Wan • ZUFEK Technical Editorial • Published October 29, 2026

Define the mating hole

Provide hole shape, dimensions, corner condition and panel thickness. Explain whether the neck is intended to enter freely or engage during installation. The behavior depends on both the neck and the surrounding material, not simply the width across flats.

Control neck depth and transition

Specify square-neck length and its relationship to the head underside. Show transitions that affect seating in the panel. A neck that is too long for the stack can interfere with the nut-side assembly even when the head and thread dimensions are correct.

Working on this type of part? Explore our custom carriage bolts and include the functional requirements in your RFQ.

Review the domed head and bearing face

Head diameter and height affect appearance, clearance and the supporting footprint. Identify visible surfaces and any finish requirements. A smooth dome does not define the underside contact, so include the seating details that matter to the connection.

Agree the finished fit

Forming variation, coating and hole condition can influence assembly. Inspect the neck in the supplied finish and define any functional fit or anti-rotation check. Do not use a visual comparison with a standard carriage bolt as the only acceptance method for a custom neck.

Specification example

When adapting a carriage bolt to a thinner panel, review neck depth and the full clamp stack. Changing only the thread length may leave the actual seating problem unresolved.

RFQ checklist

  • Mating square hole and panel thickness
  • Neck width and depth
  • Under-head transitions
  • Finished head envelope
  • Fit and anti-rotation acceptance
