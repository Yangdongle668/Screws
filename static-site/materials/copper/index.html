Copper

A material family considered where electrical or thermal function is important alongside the geometry and mechanical acceptance of the custom component.

Strength reference

Copper strength varies substantially with alloy, product form and temper. The following is a typical stock reference, not a screw acceptance requirement.

Grade / condition / basisTensile strength Rm (MPa)Yield / 0.2% stress (MPa)Scope of the reference
C11000, H02 half-hard referenceApproximately 290 typicalSpecify for the selected stockCDA design-guide stock reference; other tempers and forms differ.

Values refer to the stated material or fastener-class basis. They are not a certified property or load rating for every ZUFEK custom geometry. Confirm the applicable specification and test condition for the ordered part.

Why consider this family?

  • Relevant to electrical and thermal interface discussions
  • Grade and temper can be selected for the required component function
  • Can be reviewed for application-specific turned features

Application range

Electrical connection hardware, conductive custom components and thermal-interface threaded parts, subject to joint design.

Selection and process considerations

Conductivity and mechanical strength are separate requirements. Specify the copper grade and temper, contact surface condition, plating if required and the assembled joint requirements. Copper is not interchangeable with brass simply because both are copper-based materials.

Include in the RFQ

  • Copper grade, temper and stock form
  • Electrical or thermal requirements
  • Contact surface and finish
  • Mechanical and joint acceptance

Read the strength data in the right condition

Copper strength varies substantially with alloy, product form and temper. The following is a typical stock reference, not a screw acceptance requirement. A material stock value is not automatically the load rating of a screw or assembled joint. Keep the grade, product form, condition and applicable specification attached to the value when comparing alternatives. Review the thread engagement and mating material independently of the stock tensile figure.

Conductivity and mechanical strength are separate requirements. Specify the copper grade and temper, contact surface condition, plating if required and the assembled joint requirements. Copper is not interchangeable with brass simply because both are copper-based materials.

A practical selection and approval sequence

For Copper, first define copper grade, temper and stock form; electrical or thermal requirements; contact surface and finish; mechanical and joint acceptance. Compare those requirements with the service environment and the functional geometry. If the starting requirement is only a material family, resolve the grade and condition before comparing final quotations.

Review the chosen material with Swiss-Type & CNC Turning, Coatings & Surface Treatments, CCD Vision & Dimensional Inspection. Agree what evidence is needed at sample approval and what must accompany repeat batches. A substitute grade or condition requires approval; a similar appearance or nominal strength is not sufficient evidence of interchangeability.
