SUS304 & SUS316 Stainless Steel

Austenitic stainless choices for parts where corrosion exposure, surface condition and the specified fastener strength class need to be considered together.

Strength reference

A2/A4 groups and classes such as 70 or 80 describe finished stainless fasteners under the applicable specification. SUS304 or SUS316 alone does not establish A2-70 or A4-80 performance.

Why consider this family?

  • Useful for corrosion-exposed equipment and accessible exterior hardware
  • Grade and finish can be chosen for the actual medium
  • Commonly discussed for machined and custom-formed screw geometries

Application range

Equipment covers, electronic and optical housings, outdoor security hardware and selected fluid-handling connections.

Selection and process considerations

304 and 316 are not precipitation-hardening grades. Review the actual environment rather than assuming any stainless part is corrosion-proof. Consider thread galling, mating metals, cleaning and passivation. Form and final condition affect the mechanical requirements.

Include in the RFQ

  • 304 or 316 grade requirement
  • Finished fastener class where applicable
  • Exposure medium and mating metals
  • Surface condition, passivation and thread acceptance

Read the strength data in the right condition

A2/A4 groups and classes such as 70 or 80 describe finished stainless fasteners under the applicable specification. SUS304 or SUS316 alone does not establish A2-70 or A4-80 performance. A material stock value is not automatically the load rating of a screw or assembled joint. Keep the grade, product form, condition and applicable specification attached to the value when comparing alternatives. Review the thread engagement and mating material independently of the stock tensile figure.

A practical selection and approval sequence

For SUS304 & SUS316 Stainless Steel, first define 304 or 316 grade requirement; finished fastener class where applicable; exposure medium and mating metals; surface condition, passivation and thread acceptance. Compare those requirements with the service environment and the functional geometry. If the starting requirement is only a material family, resolve the grade and condition before comparing final quotations.

Review the chosen material with Swiss-Type & CNC Turning, Coatings & Surface Treatments, CCD Vision & Dimensional Inspection. Agree what evidence is needed at sample approval and what must accompany repeat batches. A substitute grade or condition requires approval; a similar appearance or nominal strength is not sufficient evidence of interchangeability.
