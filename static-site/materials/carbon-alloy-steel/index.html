Application range

Selection and process considerations

Steel grade, heat treatment and property class are different specifications. Review corrosion exposure, hydrogen-related process risks where applicable, coating friction and dimensional allowances. Reduced-load geometries need their own assessment.

Read the strength data in the right condition

Specify the finished fastener property class, not a strength inferred from the raw grade. Common nominal class references include 8.8, 10.9 and 12.9. A material stock value is not automatically the load rating of a screw or assembled joint. Keep the grade, product form, condition and applicable specification attached to the value when comparing alternatives. Review the thread engagement and mating material independently of the stock tensile figure.

Steel grade, heat treatment and property class are different specifications. Review corrosion exposure, hydrogen-related process risks where applicable, coating friction and dimensional allowances. Reduced-load geometries need their own assessment.

A practical selection and approval sequence

For Carbon & Alloy Steel, first define grade and final property requirement; applicable fastener specification and size; heat-treatment and coating system; functional load case and acceptance tests. Compare those requirements with the service environment and the functional geometry. If the starting requirement is only a material family, resolve the grade and condition before comparing final quotations.

Review the chosen material with Multi-Station Cold Heading, Material-Specific Heat Treatment, Coatings & Surface Treatments. Agree what evidence is needed at sample approval and what must accompany repeat batches. A substitute grade or condition requires approval; a similar appearance or nominal strength is not sufficient evidence of interchangeability.
